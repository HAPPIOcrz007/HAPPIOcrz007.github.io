Hi, these are the references and resources I use.

**Enjoy the journey and strive hard for results.**

> Do the stages in order. Skipping ahead to "harder" problems before the stage before it is
> solid is the single most common way people burn out and quit.

## a. Learning and understanding C++

Get comfortable with the language itself.

### 1. cppreference.com

![cppreference.com](img:1)

[en.cppreference.com](https://en.cppreference.com/) — a **reference**, not something to read
start to finish. Come back to it for STL behaviour, function signatures, and complexity
guarantees.

### 2. learncpp.com

![learncpp.com](img:2)

[learncpp.com](https://www.learncpp.com/) — where you actually **learn** the language: syntax,
memory, the STL. Go through it properly — everything after this depends on it.

## b. Understanding algorithms and specific practices

Build the algorithmic foundation.

### 1. USACO Guide

![USACO Guide](img:3)

[USACO Guide](https://usaco.guide/) — start at **Bronze**. Theory and practice for every topic,
a gold mine, and a reference you'll keep coming back to.

### 2. cp-algorithms.com

![cp-algorithms.com](img:4)

[cp-algorithms.com](https://cp-algorithms.com/) — the standard reference for algorithm
implementations: segment trees, DSU, number theory, and more. Come back to it whenever you need
one.

## c. Topic-wise solving

Put b's theory into practice, one topic at a time.

### 1. Codeforces topic-wise sheet

![Codeforces topic-wise sheet](img:5)

[Codeforces topic-wise sheet](https://codeforces.com/group/MWSDmqGsZm/contests) — start with
**Sheet #1**. Solve these while you're still reading through the C++ guides.

### 2. AZ101: Master C++ for DSA

![AZ101: Master C++ for DSA](img:6)

[AZ101](https://maang.in/courses/AZ101-Master-C-For-Data-Structures-and-Algorithms-67?tab=overview)
— solve **after** the sheet above to sharpen your skills. Grows linearly with your progress;
completing it may get your college to provide premium access.

## d. Mixed bag of great problems

### 1. CSES Problem Set

![CSES Problem Set](img:7)

[CSES Problem Set](https://cses.fi/problemset/) — tests whether you can recognize *which*
topic a problem needs without being told. Ranges from beginner-friendly to genuinely hard.

## e. Recommended sheets for levelling up

### 1. TLE Eliminators CP Sheet

![TLE Eliminators CP Sheet](img:8)

[TLE Eliminators CP Sheet](https://www.tle-eliminators.com/cp-sheet) — start once you've begun
giving rated Codeforces contests. Solve problems rated **+200 above your current rating** —
your long-term practice track from here on.

---

**C++ → algorithms & references → topic-wise practice → mixed practice → rating-based
levelling up.** Work through it in order, keep (a)'s and (b)'s reference links open the whole
way, and give Codeforces rounds a shot as soon as you're comfortable — contest experience under
time pressure is something no problem set can substitute for.

Good luck, and see you on the leaderboard.
